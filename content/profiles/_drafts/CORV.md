## The old story

Corvid Capital operates as an ultra-exclusive boutique advisory firm and merchant bank on Glasswater Row. While its
public brochures market bespoke counsel for cross-border mergers and private equity transactions, the institution's true
dominance lies in a far more insidious discipline. Corvid is not merely an investment bank; it is a weaponized financial
intelligence apparatus that monetizes corporate vulnerabilities before they reach the public market.

Behind fortified doors, Corvid engages in ruthless, asymmetric information arbitrage. The firm does not forecast
macroeconomic cycles; it intercepts them. Maintaining a clandestine web of corporate informants, executive
sub-contractors, and intercepted boardroom communications across every sector of the District, Corvid hoards privileged
intelligence. From hidden supply-chain bottlenecks and undisclosed regulatory probes to whispered executive panic,
Corvid maps the financial stress points of its competitors long before official disclosures are compiled.

The firm's trading desks monetize this intelligence with algorithmic zero-hesitation, deploying extreme leverage
fractions of a second before even the most well-placed corporate insiders realize the ground beneath them has shifted.
By the time an executive board convenes an emergency session, Corvid has already executed multi-leg synthetic options
strategies, cornered bespoke Credit Default Swaps (CDS), and positioned its debt advisory arm to extract extortionate
fees for managing the inevitable restructuring. Whenever corporate security teams attempt to trace these information
leaks, the forensic trail invariably dead-ends at encrypted nominee trusts and untraceable offshore shell entities.

On the exchange, CORV equity operates as an uncorrelated intelligence hedge. Because its outsized profitability relies
on exploiting the structural missteps and scandals of other market participants, the stock exhibits flat baseline
volatility during quiet periods. However, it is renowned across Glasswater Row as the market’s supreme leading
indicator of corporate catastrophe: whenever CORV suddenly spikes on anomalous volume, institutional traders brace for
an imminent corporate collapse.
