## The old story

Hummingbird Interactive hides a colossal globe spanning technology monopoly behind one of the most cheerful and
unthreatening corporate brands in history. Much like the tiny and delicate bird it is named after, the company presents
a public image of harmless and colorful agility. In reality, rather than drinking nectar, the algorithms of Hummingbird
dart through incomprehensibly massive consumer datasets, harvesting the personal behavioral data of billions to maintain
an absolute stranglehold on global targeted advertising.

What started as a simple search engine has ruthlessly expanded into an omnipresent digital surveillance apparatus. The
firm controls the underlying infrastructure of the modern age, dominating cloud computing, mobile operating systems, and
streaming video. Its hyperscale data centers are the single largest private draw on Bird Power's grid, quietly
processing the intimate daily habits, financial transactions, and geographic movements of nearly every connected citizen
in the District and a great many beyond it. Because its platforms serve as unavoidable digital utilities, the company
operates as a universal internet tollbooth, extracting immense and highly reliable cash flows from the marketing budgets
of virtually every other corporation in existence. If a business wants to be seen it must pay the Hummingbird tax.

As the largest technology company on the exchange, worth more than every other listed technology firm combined, the
stock anchors the District's entire information technology complex. It trades with an elevated beta but maintains
structurally lower volatility than smaller and riskier software peers due to its sheer gravity and monopolistic cash
reserves. Its only true existential threat is its own inescapable scale. The stock occasionally suffers violent
drawdowns whenever international regulators, sovereign governments, or historic antitrust lawsuits threaten to swat the
giant bird out of the sky and forcibly dismantle its empire.
