## The old story

Condor Extraction does not merely mine the earth. It consumes it. Condor commands a terrifying volume of the world's
base metals and rare earth elements. While boutique geological firms focus on surgical and sustainable extraction,
Condor relies entirely on overwhelming and mechanized brute force. Within the borders of the District, the company
presents a highly sanitized public image. They deploy advanced deep vein boring machines that silently siphon resources
miles beneath the surface, ensuring the glittering corporate skyline above remains completely undisturbed. However,
outside the protective legal bubble of the District, Condor functions as an unregulated empire. The firm orchestrates
enormous scale open pit strip mines that devour entire mountain ranges and literally redraw the topographical maps of
foreign nations.

Condor primarily conquers new territory using debt. Behind its towering industrial facade, the company is quietly
majority owned by LAKE and SWAN. Condor effectively functions as the physical extraction arm of their financial empires.
Whenever Lakebird or Black Swan traps a developing nation in an infrastructure loan, Condor is deployed the absolute
second that the vulnerable country defaults. Their lawyers and excavation fleets arrive simultaneously to legally seize
millions of acres of mineral rich land as collateral. If a defiant government refuses to surrender the territory or
attempts to block the machinery, Condor does not bother with diplomacy. Being the largest single client of Osprey Global
Vanguard, Condor simply authorizes an extraction contract, and Osprey forcefully removes the problem. This seamless
partnership guarantees an infinite and incredibly cheap pipeline of all types of metals from standard industrial copper
to rare earth metals like neodymium. They are the brutal physical anchor of the modern age, ensuring that the District
never experiences supply chain disruptions.

For investors on Glasswater Row, trading Condor is an exercise in extreme volatility. Maintaining a global fleet of
autonomous excavators and massive chemical refineries requires astronomical levels of continuous capital expenditure. As
a heavy industry behemoth, the stock is fiercely cyclical and trades with a high beta. The share price skyrockets
violently during District infrastructure booms or global commodity super cycles when raw materials are in desperate
demand. Conversely, the stock is terrifyingly vulnerable to sudden drawdowns. If the global economy cools, or if a
desperate foreign government attempts to nationalize Condor's sprawling overseas assets faster than Osprey can secure
them, the stock collapses under the sheer weight of its own operational costs.
