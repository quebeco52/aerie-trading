The Drainrock affair is the armed attack on Drainrock's central market and the campaign the
[[security-bureau|Security Bureau]] waged in the months after it. The campaign broke the networks that had run the ward
for a generation, followed their money into firms across the District, and made the Bureau the institution it is.

## Drainrock before

For years Drainrock was the most corrupt ward in the District. A few criminal networks ran its markets, its
moneylending and much of its property, and the company holding the ward's security concession was paid to look away.
Everyone knew it. The Bureau, then small and thought toothless, had never brought a case there that held up in court.

## The attack

On a weekday morning, with the central market at its busiest, three cars stopped outside a money changer's on the
market square. Hooded men armed with rifles got out and fired into the building, killing several people inside and
wounding more, among them shoppers in the street. They drove off before the concession's officers arrived. The money
changer had handled cash for one of the networks and was thought to have begun handling it for a rival.

## The campaign

Shootings in Drainrock were nothing new, but this one happened in daylight in front of the whole market, and most of
the dead had no part in the networks' business. The District could no longer pretend not to see. The Council pressed the
Bureau to act, and its director, Miriam Calloway, took the whole ward as a single case.

For months Bureau officers raided the networks' houses, warehouses and gambling rooms, and closed the businesses that
fronted for them. Hundreds of people were arrested. The Bureau withdrew the arms clearance of every officer on the
Drainrock concession and policed the ward itself until the Council let the concession to another company.

## The money

Ledgers seized in Drainrock led the Bureau out of the ward. The networks had washed their money through property
companies, currency dealers and the accounts of respectable firms in the inner wards and on Glasswater Row, and lent it
back out through them. Directors and lawyers who had never set foot in Drainrock were questioned, and a number of them
were charged. Firms that had banked the networks' money were wound up, and others found their banks closing their
accounts. This part of the affair, more than the raids, is what made the Bureau feared in the District's boardrooms.

## The sentences

The courts sentenced the heads of the largest networks to death and the rest of their leadership to life imprisonment.
Several of the networks never re-formed.

## What it left

The Bureau came out of Drainrock feared by the criminals it hunts and by the powerful people it investigates, and it
grew quickly in the years that followed. Organised crime in the District did not end, but what remains keeps out of
sight and away from the kind of violence that would bring the Bureau back.
