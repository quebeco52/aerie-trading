The Security Bureau is the District's state police. It was founded because the mainland's police have no authority in
the District, and the District needed a force of its own for the work that matters most to a financial centre.

## How it came to be

In the District, much of the policing is done by private security firms. The Bureau was set up to oversee their work and
coordinate between them, and to take on the work that cannot be leased out, above all organised crime.

The Bureau was controversial from the start. Many called it inefficient and toothless, especially beside the private
firms that did most of the policing. That reputation followed it for years, until the [[drainrock-affair]]. The affair
put the Bureau in the spotlight, settled its place as one of the District's essential institutions, and made it feared
both by the criminals it hunts and by the powerful people it investigates.

## Mandate

The Council's founding decree gives the Bureau three duties:

- **Organised crime.** Laundering, fraud and the networks behind them, wherever in the District's companies the money
  runs.
- **Foreign agents.** Espionage against the District's institutions and firms, and attempts to buy influence in the
  Diet or the Council.
- **Vetting.** Every officer a security company wants to arm, and every official with access to the Monetary
  Authority's or the Exchange's systems.

Its officers are armed and make their own arrests and raids, but it leaves street patrols to the municipal police and the
security companies, and has no say over who holds the concessions. Its director answers to the mandate, not to the
Council that appoints them or the Diet that funds them.

## Directors

The first director, Miriam Calloway, a prosecutor from the inner wards, served the full ten years. She led the Bureau
through the [[drainrock-affair|Drainrock affair]] and left it a force with officers in every ward. Raymond Okafor
succeeded her when her term ran out.
