The Security Bureau is the District's investigative police. The Aerie Council founded it after the Harbour Ledger
affair and names its director for a single ten-year term. The streets, the ports and the borders are policed under
concession, most of them by Bird Watch Security; the Bureau investigates.

## Why the District needed one

The District was built on the expectation that private firms would do much of what governments elsewhere do for
themselves, and policing was among the first services put out to tender. A guard on a pier can stop a thief. He cannot
follow money through forty companies registered in the free port, or tell a diplomat's driver from an intelligence
officer. For decades nobody in the District could.

A centre of world finance, tied to the mainland and answerable to it in almost nothing, has enemies abroad and
attracts the kind of money that needs a quiet place to sit. The [[harbour-ledger-affair]] showed how little the
District could do about either, and the Council founded the Bureau within the year.

## Mandate

The Council's founding decree gives the Bureau three duties:

- **Organised crime.** Laundering, fraud and the networks behind them, wherever in the District's companies the money
  runs.
- **Foreign agents.** Espionage against the District's institutions and firms, and attempts to buy influence in the
  Diet or the Council.
- **Vetting.** Every officer a security company wants to arm, and every official with access to the Monetary
  Authority's or the Exchange's systems.

It has no uniformed patrols and no say over the concessions. Its director answers to the mandate, not to the Council
that appoints them or the Diet that funds them.

## Directors

The first director, Miriam Calloway, a prosecutor from the inner wards, served the full ten years and built the Bureau
from forty investigators to some six hundred. Raymond Okafor succeeded her when her term ran out.
