The District holds three pieces of land and the water between them: a low peninsula between a broad sound to the north
and a tidal river to the south, an island off its eastern shore, and the barrier islands beyond, known in the District
as the banks, which close a wide outer sound off from the ocean. It has two frontiers on land, a line across the neck of
the peninsula and a much shorter one across the sand at the north end of the banks. Since the Charter the District has
dredged, filled and walled much of it to suit itself.

## The peninsula

The peninsula is flat country of farms, forest and marsh, crossed by rivers and drainage canals, with shallow lakes in
its interior. The frontier with the mainland runs straight across its western neck, from the head of the northern sound
to the southern river. Everything east of that line is the District's, from Glasswater Row to the farm and fishing towns
of the south shore and the ferry towns of the north shore. The far bank of the southern river, and the shore beyond it
where the river opens into the outer sound, are the mainland's, and the boundary runs down the water between.

## The island

The island lies off the peninsula's eastern shore, with a narrow sound on either side. Bridges cross both, so the road
from the peninsula to the banks runs over the island. Its harbours are home to the fishing fleets that work the sounds
and the shelf.

## The banks

The banks are a chain of narrow sand islands. The District holds them from the mouth of the northern sound south, round
the cape, to the inlet where the outer sound ends. North of the sound's mouth the banks run on as the mainland's
beaches, and the frontier crosses the dunes between them.

The banks take every hurricane first. Storms move the dunes, cut new channels and close old ones, and the
[[state-guard|State Guard]] evacuates the beach towns and resorts ahead of each one.

## The inlets

The sounds are wide and shallow, and ships reach them from the ocean only through the inlets between the islands, which
shift with the storms and are kept open by dredging. The Breakwater Trust insisted on the banks when the Charter was
drawn. Its berths could be reached from the sea only through the inlets, and a harbour whose approaches lay in the
mainland's waters would have been open on the mainland's terms. The Charter gave the District the islands, the sounds
behind them and the waters off them to the edge of the shelf, which the [[coast-guard|Coast Guard]] patrols.

## Made ground

Little of the District's shore is where the Charter found it. Iron Beak's first contract, the channel to the Breakwater
Trust's berths, set the pattern: the sand and silt dredged from the channel were pumped into the marsh beside it, behind
a ring of stone, and left to settle into the ground the port's quays and yards now stand on. Every channel since has
been dug the same way, so deepening the approaches and making new land are one job. The channel to the free port has
been deepened more than once for the largest container ships, and each deepening added land beside it. Most of the
waterfront along Glasswater Row, and the newer wards beyond it, stand on what were the estuary's tidal bays.

The sounds silt up and the channels with them, so Iron Beak's dredgers work the approaches every year. A channel left
alone for a few seasons closes.

The interior was remade earlier. Farmers drained it long before the Charter, cutting canals to the rivers and setting
pumps to lift the water out. Drained marsh soil shrinks as it dries, so the fields sink a little each year and the pumps
run a little longer.

## Sea walls

The walls came after the land. A hurricane's surge flooded the first wards built on filled marsh while the older towns,
on the higher ground behind the marshes, stayed dry, and the District has walled its shores ever since. Sea walls and
earth dikes ring the city, with gates that close the canals and creek mouths when a storm is forecast and pumping
stations behind them to lift out the rain until the gates open again. The walls face the sounds as well as the sea,
because a hurricane's winds pile the shallow water of the sounds against whichever shore they blow toward.

The banks are held another way. A wall on a sand island keeps the land behind it but loses the beach in front, so the
District pumps sand from the shelf back onto the beaches after each storm season and builds walls only in front of the
resort towns, which have chosen the hotels over the beach.

Each new scheme for made ground is contested between the Tideline Accord, which holds that every marsh filled is a
length of wall the District must later pay for, and Iron Harbor, whose members do the digging.
