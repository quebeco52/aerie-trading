## 2. Management buffer (voluntary CET1 held above the requirement)

- **Couaillier (2021), ECB WP 2618, "What are banks' actual capital targets?"** [P] Data are capital targets announced by European banks.
  - Appendix Table A1 (p.34):
    - mean announced CET1 **target 13.4%** (IQR 11.7-14.5);
    - mean CET1 **OCR 10.1%**;
    - mean P2G 0.9%;
    - mean AT1/T2 shortfall (filled with CET1) 1.0%.
  - Target minus OCR ≈ **3.3pp**. Net of P2G and the AT1/T2 shortfall, the pure voluntary buffer is ≈ **1.4pp**. These are differences of means over slightly different N, so treat them as approximate.
  - §4.1.1 (p.17): "a 1pp increase in requirements drives the target up by ∼0.3pp, implying a reduction in management buffer by ∼0.7pp". The buffer absorbs most of a hike in the short run. Banks also treat P2G as if it were a requirement.
  - p.26: a 1pp requirement hike leads to −0.07pp quarterly NFC loan growth, about −2.8pp annual.
- **Bridges et al. (2014), BoE WP 486, Table C (p.17)** [P]: after a +1pp requirement, banks' actual capital ratio rises 0.41pp after 1 year and 0.95pp after 3 years. The buffer is "fully restored in less than four years", so the long-run pass-through is about 1:1.
- **Per-bank headroom at end-2024** (actual CET1 minus requirement incl. CCyB) [P], from the table above:
  - JPM 3.4, HSBC 3.8, UBS 3.8, RBC 1.6, CBA 2.0, MUFG 5.2, DBS 7.8, HBAP 6.5
  - SEB 3.4, DNB 4.1, Danske 3.2, ING 2.8, DB 2.6, BNP 2.5, BoI 3.3, Arion 2.9, BCEE ≈14 (outlier, non-consolidated).
  - **Median across all 17 banks: 3.4pp**, IQR ≈2.8-4.1 (my arithmetic). Undisclosed P2G (ECB average 1.3) and undisclosed P2 sit inside this headroom, so the purely voluntary part is smaller, roughly 1.5-2pp. That matches Couaillier's ≈1.4pp.
  - The headroom for JP/AU/SG/HK is overstated because their Pillar 2 add-ons are unpublished.
- Fed Supervision & Regulation Report, Nov 2024 [P]: aggregate large-bank CET1 ratio "approximately 13 percent" at 2024Q3. No requirement figure is given.

- **ECB SREP 2024 aggregate** [P]: the average CET1 ratio was 15.81% (2024Q2, transitional) against an OCR excl P2G of ≈9.9%, a gap of ≈5.9pp. Against OCR+P2G of 11.2% the gap is ≈4.6pp. These subtractions are mine, and the dates differ slightly (ratio at Q2-2024, requirements for 2024).

## 5. Does WHO heads the regulator change the requirement?

- **US Basel III endgame NPR (Barr era), 88 FR 64028 (18 Sep 2023)** [P], at 64168:
  - The proposal would raise the binding CET1 requirement of large holding companies by "around **16 percent**".
  - Fn 464 breaks this down: **19%** for Category I-II (GSIBs and similar), 6% for domestic Cat III-IV, 14% for foreign IHCs, and 9% at depository institutions.
  - Barr (speech, 10 Jul 2023) [P]: this is "equivalent to requiring the largest banks hold an additional 2 percentage points of capital".
- **Barr's re-proposal outline (speech, 10 Sep 2024)** [P]:
  - GSIB aggregate CET1 requirement +**9%**, roughly half the original.
  - Non-GSIB large banks +3-4% (from AOCI) and +0.5% otherwise.
  - The re-proposal was never finalised.
- **Quarles tailoring (Fed press release, 10 Oct 2019)** [P]:
  - −**0.6%** aggregate required capital and −2% required liquid assets for banks ≥$100bn.
  - The release states there is no reduction for GSIBs.
- **Bowman-era eSLR final rule, 90 FR 55248 (1 Dec 2025)** [P], at 55270, Table 8:
  - Tier 1 requirement −$13bn (<2%) at GSIB holding companies and **−$219bn (−28%)** at their bank subsidiaries.
  - This is a leverage (Tier 1) measure, not risk-based CET1.
  - Net swing in GSIB CET1 requirements between the two VCS stances: about +19% proposed (Barr), then a 9% re-proposal, then never adopted. Above the SLR backstop the change was ≈0%.
- **Agarwal, Lucca, Seru & Trebbi (2014), QJE 129(2):889-938** [P, read in NBER WP 17736 version; QJE table numbering may differ]:
  - Identification: state-chartered banks rotate between state and federal examiners on a fixed schedule.
  - When the Fed is lead, the composite CAMELS rating is **+0.096** (≈ ⅓ of the within-bank SD of 0.295). When the FDIC is lead, **+0.072** (≈ ⅕ of SD 0.389). Source: Tables 3-4.
  - Federal examiners downgrade about **twice as often**: 76% vs 24% of SMB downgrades, and 65% vs 35% of NMB downgrades (Table 5).
  - Under a federal examiner, Tier 1 RBCR is **+0.20pp** (Fed) and +0.06pp (FDIC), and ROA is lower (Table 7).
  - A 1-SD wider federal-state spread (0.07) is associated with ≈ **+40% bank-failure rate** in a state (p.23).
- **Supervisor "type" distribution**: I found no Bordo-Istrefi-style hawk/dove classification of bank supervisors.
  - Closest proxies: the federal-vs-state examiner strictness above; Fed Board dissents on rules (Brainard dissented on 20+ deregulatory actions in 2019-20 [S, press/advocacy sources]); and the US VCS sequence Quarles → Barr → Bowman (anecdotal, not a measured distribution).
  - For the game, a 3-type stance (strict / middle / light) would have to be calibrated to the requirement range in Q1, not to a published distribution.

## 6. Jordà, Richter, Schularick & Taylor, "Bank Capital Redux" (REStud 2021; read in NBER WP 23287, rev. July 2020) [P]

- **Capital ratio = book equity / TOTAL assets (unweighted, leverage-style)**, not CET1/RWA (p.7). Converting needs RWA density (Q7).
- **Crisis probability**, Table 4 (p.16) probit:
  - Full sample: the capital-ratio coefficient is **+0.17*** **, i.e. the wrong sign.
  - Post-WW2: +0.06 (n.s.). The change in the capital ratio is insignificant.
  - The abstract reads "no association between higher capital and lower risk of banking crisis".
- **Severity**, Table 8 (p.27):
  - The financial-recession × capital-ratio coefficient on cumulative 5-year real GDP per capita is **0.86 (s.e. 0.42, p<0.10)** per 1pp of capital ratio.
  - Year-5 level effect: 0.31** per pp.
  - For normal recessions the effect is −0.10 (n.s.).
  - Text p.26: +10pp of capital gives +3.1% GDP in year 5 and +8.6% cumulative.
  - This confirms the brief's 0.86 figure.

## 8. Capital requirements push lending to nonbanks

**Irani, Iyer, Meisenzahl & Peydró (2021), RFS 34(5):2181-2235** [P, read in author's version; page numbers may differ from the RFS version]. US syndicated loans (SNC register), 1993-2014.

- **Table 5, col. [1]** (nonbank share of loan on syndicate-mean Tier 1/RWA): coefficient **−1.547*****. So 1pp lower syndicate Tier 1 ratio means **+1.55pp nonbank share**. A 1-SD capital fall (2.1pp) gives **+3.25pp**, which is 14.1% of the mean nonbank share of 23.1%.
- **Basel III shock, fn 33 (p.24)**: +1pp capital requirement leads to a **+9.5pp** syndicate-level nonbank share (intensive margin), +0.15pp bank capital after 1 year, and +0.40pp loan-sale probability.
- The same footnote cites Berrospide & Edge (2016): +1pp requirement gives −1.4pp bank-level C&I loan growth [S].

## 9. The build-up squeeze, refitted (2026-10-04)

**Macroeconomic Assessment Group (2010), Interim Report (FSB/BCBS, Aug 2010)** [P, read in full text]. Per +1pp target capital ratio:
- All models, 4-year phase-in: GDP **−0.19%** at 18 quarters (−0.16 national + 0.03 IMF spillovers); range 0.07-0.31 excluding the top and bottom fifths. Lending spreads **+15bp**, lending volumes **−1.4%** at 18q and −1.9% at the end of the simulation (seven of nine models: 0.7-2.1%) (pp. 2, 20).
- Models with lending standards (VAR add-factors on investment and consumption): **−0.32%** at 18q with no policy response, **−0.17%** with the central bank responding. Same-member comparison: standards add 0.15pp over spreads only (pp. 21-22, fn 19).
- A 2-year phase-in gives a slightly larger trough, sooner (10q). GDP is back to about −0.10% eight years on, with no overshoot (p. 3).
- Investment is hit harder than consumption (ECB MCM: −0.38% vs −0.06%) (p. 20).
- UK FSA: banks meet a higher target about half through capital and half through RWA, and half of the RWA cut is lending (p. 15).
- Final Report (Dec 2010): the maximum GDP impact is 0.17% per pp.

**Bridges et al. (2014), Table D** [P]: year-1 loan growth per +1pp is secured household −0.94 (peak −0.77 in Q1), unsecured −0.68 (n.s.), CRE −8.07, non-CRE PNFC −3.86. Secured lending "lasts less than a year". §6.3: increases drive the CRE and secured responses, and secured lending is "not significantly affected by reductions".

**Bassett, Chosak, Driscoll & Zakrajšek (2014)** [P, FEDS 2012-24]: a 1 SD credit supply shock gives core lending capacity −4% (permanent) and real GDP −0.75% at 2 years. GDP/lending ≈ 0.19 (MAG's ratio is 0.14).

**Built** (CreditFiscalSubsystem):
- **Capital required** = requirement + CCyB. MAG: "any increase in target capital ratios whether its source be higher regulatory minima, required buffers…".
- **Households:** a rise in required capital cuts household debt to income at once by 0.70 × 0.94 + 0.30 × 0.68 = 0.862% per pp (the stock's mortgage share). A cut lends nothing back.
- **Lending standards:** the shortfall against required capital (built tracks required at τ 1.52y) feeds lending standards at `SLOOS_CAPITAL_SHORTFALL_PREMIUM_EQUIVALENT` **0.87**, fitted to MAG standards+policy −0.17% at 18q (4-year linear phase-in).
- **Buffer:** the old permanent CCyB-level term (0.22, uncited) is removed.
