# Sovereign Reserve Fund — evidence base

Compiled 2026-10-04 from fund annual reports, policy pages, statutes and papers (six parallel research passes, each source read on the web).

**Tags.** [P] = number read in the primary document (fund report / official page / statute / paper). [S] = second-hand (news, search snippet, blog). "p." = printed page unless "PDF p." is given. Computed values show their arithmetic; nothing was rounded silently (where a computed value is rounded the unrounded figure is shown).

**Consistency year.** Q1 uses the policy *in force during 2024 / FY2024-25* unless the row says otherwise. Later changes (2025-26) are noted separately so the 2024 cross-section stays clean.

### Norway GPFG 40 → 60 (2007-2009)
- Proposed in St.meld. nr. 24 (2006–2007): 60 target, 50-70 interval; committee (Innst. S. nr. 228 §3.2.2): "tilpasningen kan ta flere år". Storting adopted 12 Jun 2007 unanimously; a Progress Party 40%-equity motion lost 82–23 [P] Storting minutes s070612 pp.3549-3550.
- [P] NBIM AR 2009 PDF p.14: "The ministry decided in June 2007 that 60 percent … a target that was reached in June 2009." → **~24 months**.
- Phase-in money: NOK 1,010bn of equities bought Jun 2007–Jun 2009 (641bn new inflows + 369bn coupons/maturities/bond sales) [P] PDF p.22. 2009 leg: 49.6 (end-2008) → 60 (June 2009) = +10.4pp in ~6 months, NOK 275bn bought (PDF pp.26, 30) — the post-Lehman drawdown was bought into.

### Norway GPFG 62.5 → 70 (2017-2019)
- Norges Bank advised 75% (letter 1 Dec 2016) [P]; Mork commission: all but the chair recommended 70 [P] Innst. 357 S.
- Meld. St. 26 (2016–2017) submitted 31 Mar 2017; Storting adopted **6 Jun 2017** [P] stortinget.no sak 67718. Ministry of Finance phase-in letter 22 Sep 2017: "over a period of 20 months from September 2017 to April 2019" [P] NBIM letter 24 Aug 2019; effective 70/30 from 1 May 2019.
- Because the actual benchmark was already 66.2 vs strategic 62.5, no trades were needed until Oct 2018; upward adjustments ≈ NOK 500bn; model transaction costs ≈ NOK 630m; relative-return effect ≈ −2bp (≈ NOK 2bn) [P] same letter.
- Decision chain: Norges Bank advises → Ministry proposes in a white paper → Storting endorses → Ministry sets the percentage in the mandate (no separate statutory vote).

### Price effect of the 31 Oct 2014 announcement
- Same day: BoJ (5–4 vote) raised the monetary-base pace to ~¥80tn/yr and tripled ETF buying to ~¥3tn/yr [P] BoJ statement k141031a. The GPIF news was reported during the session [S, CNBC/Reuters]. Nikkei 225 +4.8% to 16,413.76, highest close since Nov 2007 [S, Business Standard]; TOPIX "over 4 percent higher" [S, CNBC]. **The two shocks cannot be separated in daily data.**
- No peer-reviewed GPIF-specific estimate found. Practitioner only: Teneo/Kensho via Institutional Investor (22 Apr 2015) — across 19 GPIF statements "the Nikkei 225 rose by an average of 1.17 percent in the day following an announcement" [P article, not peer-reviewed].
- Comparators for price impact of large public buying of home equity [P]:
  - Barbon & Gianinazzi (2019), *Review of Asset Pricing Studies* 9(2):210-255: "long-run demand curves for stocks are downward sloping with unitary price elasticity"; draft: "20 basis points increase in aggregate market valuation per trillion Yen" (lower bound).
  - Harada & Okimoto (2021), *Int. Review of Financial Analysis* 77, 101826 (RIETI DP 19-E-014): Nikkei 225 stocks +0.0554% per ¥10bn of BoJ ETF purchases in QQE1, +0.0195% in QQE4 (Table 6, PDF p.15); cumulative ≈20% by Oct 2017.
  - Charoenwong, Morck & Wiwattanakantang, NBER WP 25525 (2019): stock-return elasticity "around 2"; purchases of 1% of assets ↔ 1% valuation rise over a quarter.
  - Gabaix & Koijen, NBER WP 28967 (2021): "$1 in the stock market increases the market's aggregate value by about $5" (price elasticity 0.2).
  - Harvey, Mazzoleni & Melone, NBER WP 33554 (2025): rebalancing out of overweight stocks lowers next-day equity returns by 17bp.
  - Not read (paywalled): "Institutional stock-bond portfolios rebalancing and financial stability: Norway as a case study", *Applied Economics* 58(17); "Pension Fund Trading Behavior and the Index Inclusion: Evidence from the Korean Stock Market", *EMFT* 59(3).
